Weekly Tips




THE WEEKEND 28 – 29th MARCH IN FLORENCE




MUSEUMS, EVENTS and MARKETS:


Rothko in Florence: The Fondazione Palazzo Strozzi is presenting an unmissable exhibition dedicated to the great American art master Mark Rothko. The show explores the evolution of his work: from his early figurative paintings, influenced by Expressionism and Surrealism, to the famous abstract canvases of the 1950s and 1960s, also highlighting his connection with the Italian artistic tradition.

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec: the Museo degli Innocenti takes you on an immersive journey into fin-de-siècle Paris, featuring iconic posters, colorful lithographs, and illustrations by the French master Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec.

Half Marathon Florence: on Sunday there’s a charity run set against the charm, history, and art of Florence, happening at the same time in over 100 cities in Italy and abroad.

Arts & Crafts: in Piazza Santa Maria Novella, the market exhibition is back to celebrate the Florentine New Year—perfect for diving into local craftsmanship and traditions.

Florence Flower Show: this weekend the Corsini Garden hosts some of the best plant growers and artisans in Italy, showcasing rare and unusual plants from all over the world.

Horror Festival: the Visarno Hippodrome turns into a dark universe—monsters and fears come to life through immersive experiences, shows, performance art, and all things macabre.

Creative Mani Factory: at Manifattura Tabacchi there’s a weekend artisan market—perfect if you’re into independent design, handmade goods, and limited-edition pieces (10am–8pm).

Il Gusto Toscano: at Conventino Fuori le Mura, enjoy a weekend full of great wine, tasty food, tastings, and handmade Tuscan products (11am–8pm).

Wonder and Pick: in Scandicci and Lastra a Signa you can wander through fields of tulips—over 150,000 flowers across 65 varieties—and even pick your own.

Fiera dei Quaresimali: the countdown to Easter begins with these traditional fairs, filling the Cascine every Sunday for four weeks with hundreds of stalls stretching over 1.5 km (8am–7:30pm).

Borgo DiVino: stop by Scandicci for this wine festival with over 90 producers and more than 500 wines to taste.

Firenze in Arte: back in the heart of Oltrarno, in Piazza del Carmine, this market exhibition celebrates artistic and traditional craftsmanship—from leather goods to ceramics, jewelry, painting, clothing, and bookbinding (10am–8pm).

Record, Comics & Vintage Fair: in Piazza dei Ciompi you’ll find records, vinyls, comics, games, toys, plus vintage objects and clothing.
