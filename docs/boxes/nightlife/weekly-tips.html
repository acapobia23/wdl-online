Weekly Tips




TIPS FOR THE WEEKEND 09-10th MAY IN FLORENCE




EVENTS, MUSEUMS, AND GARDENS:


The Arno: to discover Florence from a different perspective and literally walk on the river waters, we recommend: a ride on the historic Renaioli boat (www.renaioli.it), paddling under Ponte Vecchio on a SUP board (www.toscanasup.org), and for the more adventurous, a rafting excursion (www.firenzerafting.it).

Gardens to visit in spring: the Iris Garden reopens, where around 3,000 varieties of hybrid irises from all over the world bloom; the panoramic Rose Garden oasis, home to more than 1,000 plant varieties including 350 ancient roses; the charming 19th-century English-style romantic park of the Stibbert Museum Garden, with caves, terracotta statues, and Egyptian and Hellenistic temples; and finally the Medici Park of Pratolino, filled with grottos, statues, fountains, and water features.

Exhibitions you shouldn't miss: at Palazzo Strozzi, the major exhibition dedicated to Rothko, plus in the courtyard you'll find the installation There are other fish in the sea by Superflex, reflecting on climate change; the Museo Novecento presents Baselitz. AVANTI!, a major exhibition dedicated to Georg Baselitz, one of the leading figures in contemporary art; at Palazzo Medici Riccardi, the exhibition Firenze Decò. Atmospheres of the 1920s will be on display; and finally, it's the last weekend to experience at the Cattedrale dell'Immagine the 35-minute immersive journey into the artistic and personal world of Frida Kahlo. At Villa Bardini, you can also visit Florence '50s '60s '70s, a photographic journey through three decades of Florentine history featuring images from the Foto Locchi Archive.



MARKETS AND EVENTS:


Creative Factory: this weekend in Piazza dei Ciompi, a celebration of creativity and handmade passion with artisans, makers, and designers (H 9:30–20).

Rare Market: set in the beautiful Visarno Racecourse, this spring market returns with a wide and carefully selected mix of vintage, handmade goods, mid-century pieces, illustrations, vinyl records, and design items (H 11–20).

Piantala: the largest plant pop-up sale arrives at Manifattura Tabacchi from Friday to Sunday, featuring more than 15,000 plants across 200 different varieties (H 10–20).

Ognissanti in Festa: on Saturday, Piazza Ognissanti hosts a market exhibition dedicated to vintage collectibles and creative crafts — unique finds, handmade creations, and a nostalgic journey through memories and creativity (H 9–20).

Fiera d'Oltrarno: on Sunday, the square fills with stalls selling crafts, antiques, books, clothing, and vintage treasures, alongside local specialties such as honey, truffles, and much more (9am–7pm).

Fierucolina del Carmine: Sunday morning market in Piazza del Carmine, perfect for local food shopping with products from small producers including bread, pasta, vegetables, fresh olive oil, wine, fruit, honey, flour, and cheese (H 8–13).

Only Usato Market: on Sunday at Conventino Letterario, the event dedicated to second-hand and vintage shopping returns, with market stalls, artisan workshops open to visitors, and plenty of music (H 12–22).

Around Florence: on Sunday, the streets of Empoli's historic center will host Empolissima di Primavera, a huge market with clothing, food stands, and just about everything else imaginable. Festa Rinascimentale in Pontassieve celebrates the area's historical roots with parades, performances, and traditional food experiences. Barberino in Fiore: the ancient medieval village will bloom with plants and flowers, where the colors of roses and seasonal blossoms blend with wine tastings and the flavors of the best local products, while the creativity of artisan masters meets the genius of past and contemporary artists. Festa del Contadino in Vinci offers the chance to rediscover rural culture and local traditions.
