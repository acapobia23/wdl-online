Weekly Tips




TIPS FOR THE LONG WEEKEND 01-03th MAY IN FLORENCE




EVENTS, MUSEUMS, AND GARDENS:


Sunday at the Museum: like every first Sunday of the month, state museums are free in Florence—from the Uffizi Galleries to the National Museum of the Bargello, plus many lesser-known spots that are all worth discovering.

Gardens to visit in spring: the Iris Garden reopens, showcasing around 3,000 hybrid iris varieties from all over the world; at Villa Bardini you can admire Florence's main landmarks surrounded by the scent and beauty of wisteria, with stunning lilac, purple, and pink blooms stretching 70 meters long and 4.5 meters wide; the panoramic Rose Garden features over 1,000 plant varieties, including 350 ancient roses; the romantic 19th-century English-style park of the Stibbert Museum Garden includes grottos, terracotta statues, and Egyptian and Hellenistic-style temples; finally, the Medici Park of Pratolino is full of grottos, statues, fountains, and water features.

Exhibitions not to miss: at Palazzo Strozzi there's a major exhibition dedicated to Mark Rothko, and in the courtyard you'll find the installation "There are other fish in the sea" by Superflex reflecting on climate change; the Museo del Novecento presents Baselitz. AVANTI!, a major show dedicated to Georg Baselitz, one of the leading figures in contemporary art; at Palazzo Medici Riccardi there's the exhibition Firenze Decò. Atmosfere degli Anni 20; and finally, it's the last weekend to see the immersive 35-minute experience at the Cattedrale dell'Immagine, which takes visitors into the artistic and personal world of Frida Kahlo.



MARKETS AND EVENTS:


Celebrating May 1st (Labour Day): Fiera del Primo Maggio at the Parco delle Cascine — for over 30 years, this open-air fair brings together 200+ vendors with everything from clothing and home goods to accessories and food. Vivo – Festa del lavoro creativo at the Ippodromo del Visarno — a full day surrounded by greenery with music (by Tenax Club), a creative market, street food, and a kids area (from 11:00). Mercato Vintage in Piazza Mino — a nice spot to browse collectibles, crafts, and more.

MIDA – International Crafts Fair: Until May 3rd at the Fortezza da Basso, where tradition meets innovation—expect handmade creations from some of the world's top artisans (H 10-22).

Plant & Flower Market: Running through Sunday at the Giardino dell'Orticoltura, with a huge variety of plants and flowers from over 75 exhibitors across Italy (H 9-19:30).

French Regional Market: Back for the long weekend in Piazza Santa Croce, bringing all the flavors, scents, and colors of France—lots of traditional food specialties (H 9-21).

Ars Manualis – The heritage of craftsmanship: At Piazzale Michelangelo, an arts & crafts market showcasing high-level handmade work (Friday & Saturday, H 10-20).

Savonarola Antiquaria: On Sunday in Piazza Savonarola, perfect for hunting vintage treasures, antiques, and unique finds (H 8–20).

Campo di Marte in Fiera: On Sunday around the Stadio Artemio Franchi, a large market with all kinds of goods—from clothes and plants to food and more (H 8-20).

Vino al Vino in Cantina: In Panzano in Chianti over the weekend, you can meet local producers and visit 20 participating wineries (H 11-18:30).
